The current kernel uses cached-distance graph planes, high-bit NEW/OLD tags, bounded forward and reverse samples (`p≤16`), NEW–NEW and NEW–OLD reciprocal proposals, target-owned deterministic merges, and exact changed-edge/pending-NEW termination accounting. It rejects non-finite inputs and join overflow without publishing a graph. Both fixtures exhausted the requested iteration budget, so their quality gains are not convergence claims. The reported byte count is the implementation's simultaneously owned device allocations, not peak process RSS.

The richer join costs more than the earlier outgoing-only checkpoint at these isolated points. That trade is accepted provisionally because sampled overlap improved by 24.61 and 59.18 percentage points respectively; end-to-end CAGRA quality and scale still decide whether the design is retained. Five-process ranges retain initialization and scheduling cost rather than presenting only an in-process warm result.

The first downstream SIFT1M gate used the previous initializer. AUTO CAGRA construction completed in an observed 830.0 s and ended with CPU prune, but CAGRA recall@10 was 0.4057 versus hnswlib 0.8903 at the nominal public M=16/effort=32 point; the graph topologies are not identical. The build time was collected under substantial unrelated iGPU load and is not an isolated performance baseline; the quality failure is valid. The gate predates the query-content seed fix, host detour-rank/reverse-interleave optimizer, and this NEW/OLD/reverse rewrite. It has not been rerun, so 0.4057 remains the latest measured failure rather than a current-code recall claim.

Three current-code SIFT100K prefix preflights completed AUTO construction in 8.672–8.800 s (8.686 s median); the full isolated CAGRA worker peaked at 398.2–398.5 MiB. Downstream FORCE_GPU recall@10 was exactly 0.9718 in every run versus hnswlib 0.9504–0.9525 at M=16/effort=32. This demonstrates practical 100K construction and good intermediate recall; it does not establish convergence, device-only peak allocation, or SIFT1M quality. The build still ends in the CPU host optimizer, and hnswlib remained 5.16× faster to build by the three-run medians.

SIFT1M/K64 remains a scale risk even without `N²` storage. Estimated owned device allocations are 1,197,748,744 bytes (1.116 GiB) with a device-accessible dataset and 1,709,748,744 bytes (1.592 GiB) when the 512 MB dataset is staged. A conservative per-iteration upper bound is 1.52 billion pair distances, 194.56 billion dimension visits at D=128, and 192 million proposals, followed by serial `O(K)` target-row merges. Those are design bounds, not measured consumption or throughput. Run a controlled intermediate-scale preflight before repeating the full gate; peak process memory, SIFT1M practicality, and full FORCE_GPU CAGRA construction remain unproven.
