The current kernel uses cached-distance graph planes, high-bit NEW/OLD tags, bounded forward and reverse samples (`p≤16`), NEW–NEW and NEW–OLD reciprocal proposals, target-owned deterministic merges, and exact changed-edge/pending-NEW termination accounting. It rejects non-finite inputs and join overflow without publishing a graph. Both fixtures exhausted the requested iteration budget, so their quality gains are not convergence claims. The reported byte count is the implementation's simultaneously owned device allocations, not peak process RSS.

The richer join costs more than the earlier outgoing-only checkpoint at these isolated points. That trade is accepted provisionally because sampled overlap improved by 24.61 and 59.18 percentage points respectively; end-to-end CAGRA quality and scale still decide whether the design is retained. Five-process ranges retain initialization and scheduling cost rather than presenting only an in-process warm result.

The first downstream SIFT1M gate used the previous initializer. AUTO CAGRA construction completed in an observed 830.0 s and ended with CPU prune, but CAGRA recall@10 was 0.4057 versus hnswlib 0.8903 at the nominal public M=16/effort=32 point. That valid historical failure predates the query-content seed fix, host detour-rank/reverse-interleave optimizer, and this NEW/OLD/reverse rewrite.

Three clean, DLL-bound SIFT100K prefix preflights completed AUTO construction in 8.904–9.087 s (9.084 s median); the full isolated CAGRA worker peaked at 398.8–398.9 MiB. Downstream FORCE_GPU recall@10 was exactly 0.9718 in every run versus hnswlib 0.9503–0.9538 at M=16/effort=32. This demonstrates practical 100K construction and good intermediate recall; it does not establish convergence or close the full SIFT1M product gate. The build still ends in the CPU host optimizer, and hnswlib remained 5.57× faster to build by the three-run medians.

Three clean current-code SIFT1M gates completed AUTO construction in 99.874–100.525 s (100.093 s median); the isolated CAGRA worker had a 2,343.2 MiB median peak. Downstream CAGRA recall@10 was exactly 0.9036 versus hnswlib 0.8899–0.8915, passing the maximum 0.0200 closeness gap in every process. This establishes full-scale practicality and closes the CAGRA-vs-hnswlib matched recall gate for the current initializer/optimizer combination. It does not close T12.5's comparison against IVF-PQ initialization or prove standalone convergence, and the final host optimizer still prevents a full FORCE_GPU build claim. hnswlib's median build was 76.869 s and median peak was 1,387.0 MiB, so product performance remains negative.

The frozen V1 telemetry attributes a median 90.444 s (90.36% of complete build) to GPU NN-Descent and 9.034 s to host optimizer/prune/merge. Each SIFT1M process reports 1,098 kernels, 2,458 submissions, 1,820 literal waits, 459 D2H calls carrying 167.85 MiB, zero H2D calls, and 653.98 MiB peak algorithm-owned USM. All runs exhaust six iterations with about 18.5% of intermediate edges still changed/pending. Synchronization/readback reduction in the GPU initializer is therefore the first measured optimization target; host prune is second. Full artifacts and hashes: `tables/arrow-lake/cagra-build-v1.md`.

The first bounded synchronization experiment kept active counts on-device and launched fixed-`N` consumers. It reduced SIFT1M D2H calls 459→21, submissions 2,458→2,020, and waits 1,820→488, but regressed complete build 8.91% and initializer wall 9.85% in a fresh parent/candidate pair. The patch was rejected and production source restored. The next experiment must preserve dynamic consumer ranges and reduce only the large convergence readbacks. Evidence: `tables/arrow-lake/nndescent-active-count-v1.md`.

That convergence-only follow-up preserved dynamic ranges and cut SIFT1M D2H bytes 176,003,272→128,003,416, but its single-work-group reduction regressed complete build 2.14% and initializer wall 1.91%. It too was rejected and restored. The next bounded target is the repeated `heads[N]` scratch fill: initialize once per iteration and reset only rows touched by each unique target-owned consumer. Evidence: `tables/arrow-lake/nndescent-convergence-reduction-v1.md`.

SIFT1M/K64 remains a scale risk even without `N²` storage. Estimated owned device allocations are 1,197,748,744 bytes (1.116 GiB) with a device-accessible dataset and 1,709,748,744 bytes (1.592 GiB) when the 512 MB dataset is staged. A conservative per-iteration upper bound is 1.52 billion pair distances, 194.56 billion dimension visits at D=128, and 192 million proposals, followed by serial `O(K)` target-row merges. Those are design bounds, not measured consumption or throughput. The measured default degree-16/intermediate-32 CAGRA path is practical at SIFT1M, but K64 and full FORCE_GPU construction remain unproven.
